How a digital protest works

Four questions decide whether a worldwide protest is real or theatre. Here is how each one is answered.

1 Same moment

A protest opens with a single start and end time. Everyone joins that one window, in every timezone, so presence is simultaneous rather than spread out.

2 No travel

Taking part from home counts fully. Nobody is asked to gather, march or go anywhere.

3 Any connection

Join over the internet, a nearby Wi-Fi hotspot, a Bluetooth beacon, a scanned QR code, or fully offline — the join is queued and counted when it syncs.

4 One protocol

Peaceful conduct is an entry requirement, not a request. The same rules bind every participant, in every country, with no local exceptions.

What we never collect

This is the whole data model, not a summary of it.

Your real name never asked
Email or phone never asked
Street address never asked
Photos or ID never asked

What you do give

Age range one choice
Gender one choice
Country / city / area for the count

You are given a generated protester name when you join, such as Quiet Willow #4192. It is not linked to anything about you.

Physical presence is separate, verified and optional

Digital presence is never a stepping stone to being asked to attend something.

  1. You request physical presence — no obligation, no pressure.
  2. Verification runs before anything is scheduled.
  3. A short list is drawn from different areas, never one dense crowd.
  4. The same protocol applies to every scheduled group.
